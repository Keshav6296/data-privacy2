# Practical 6: Privacy Policy Analysis of Instagram

## Aim
Review the current public policy for clarity about collection, purpose, sharing, security, retention, and user choices.

## Method
Use the Instagram/Meta Privacy Policy and Privacy Center. Note the policy version or access date because regional wording and settings may change. This review does not verify internal implementation [1][2].

## Checklist and student observations
| Topic | Questions | Observation to record |
|---|---|---|
| Data collected | Does the notice distinguish information users provide, activity, device information, and partner sources? | Meta describes broad categories. Map each category to a stated purpose. |
| Purpose | Are service delivery, safety, analytics, personalization, and advertising explained? | Note whether a typical user can understand why each category is used. |
| Sharing | Does it explain sharing within Meta, with service providers, and with other parties? | Record stated categories; do not guess recipients not named in the text. |
| Retention | How long is each category kept and why? | Flag broad or purpose-based language that needs clarification. |
| Tracking | Are cookies/device signals and controls explained across app and web? | Test whether settings are findable in both contexts. |
| User rights | Can users access, download, correct, delete, or manage preferences? | Accounts Center includes information tools; verify current steps directly [3]. |
| Children | Are age-related defaults and safeguards clear? | Compare current materials to historic DPC findings without implying they describe current settings [4]. |

## Scoring rubric
Score each topic from 1 (unclear or absent) to 5 (clear, specific, and easy to act on). Cite a section or example for each score. Mentioning a topic is not enough; consider whether a typical user can understand the practical effect.

## Recommendations
Summarize three strengths and three areas for clarification. Consider a short data-use summary, purpose-linked settings, clear retention examples, an understandable recipient/processor explanation, and an easy route to access or delete account data.

## Conclusion
Policy text is evidence of disclosure, not proof a practice is lawful, proportionate, or implemented effectively. Review clarity and test whether controls are usable.
