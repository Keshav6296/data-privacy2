# Practical 3: Privacy Regulation Compliance Assessment

## Aim
Map privacy requirements that may apply to an Instagram data activity and identify evidence needed before making a compliance conclusion.

## Scope and limitation
Applicable law depends on user location, processing purpose, service entity, and territorial criteria. This is a student mapping exercise, not legal advice or a finding that Meta is compliant or non-compliant.

## Frameworks to consider
- **GDPR:** may apply to processing of personal data of people in the EU/EEA when territorial criteria are met. Relevant themes include transparency, purpose limitation, minimization, security, data-subject rights, accountability, and child protection [1].
- **CCPA/CPRA:** may grant rights to eligible California residents and duties for covered businesses; coverage depends on statutory definitions and exceptions [2].
- **India DPDP Act 2023 and Rules 2025:** may apply where territorial scope is met. India notified phased commencement on 13 Nov 2025. The Gazette assigns different dates, including an 18-month phase for many core provisions. Verify the official timeline at submission rather than assuming all duties have commenced [3][4][5].

## Requirement-to-evidence matrix
| Topic | Evidence to review | Classroom status |
|---|---|---|
| Notice and transparency | Current notice, layered explanations, version history | Public policy exists; readability needs user testing |
| Purpose and minimization | Data inventory, purpose-to-field map, retention schedule | Internal evidence unavailable; necessity not verified |
| User rights | Access, correction, deletion, opt-out workflows and logs | Controls described; completion time not tested |
| Children and defaults | Age controls, privacy defaults, impact assessments | Historic Irish DPC inquiry found issues in the period examined; this does not establish present settings [6] |
| Security/accountability | Access reviews, risk records, incident logs, processor contracts | Not verifiable from policy alone |
| Processors/transfers | Agreements, transfer assessments, subprocessor list | Public materials do not prove operational compliance |

## Assessment method
For every purpose, record the applicable rule, evidence, gap, risk, owner, and remediation date. Mark items “not verified” where internal evidence is unavailable. Recheck official texts and effective dates.

## Conclusion
A privacy policy is only one piece of compliance evidence. A defensible assessment also needs operational records, rights-request testing, contracts, security evidence, and jurisdiction-specific review. Public materials support issue-spotting, not a definitive compliance score.
