# Practical 9: Ethical Considerations in Data Privacy

## Aim
Apply ethical principles to social-platform data practices and recommend choices that respect expectations, agency, and safety.

## Principles
- **Transparency:** explain what is collected, why, and who receives it in plain language.
- **Meaningful choice:** do not make optional processing appear necessary; make withdrawal usable.
- **Data minimization:** avoid signals that are not needed for a defined purpose.
- **Purpose limitation:** avoid unexpected reuse of information.
- **Fairness:** examine whether ranking, recommendation, or moderation disadvantages groups.
- **Safety and dignity:** protect children and people at heightened risk; reduce unwanted exposure.
- **Accountability:** document decisions, measure outcomes, and provide ways to challenge harmful errors.

## Ethical dilemma exercise
A recommendation feature may improve discovery by using viewing and interaction history. Personalization can be useful, but behavioral signals can expose sensitive interests or amplify harmful material. Ask whether the collection is necessary, whether people understand it, and whether a less intrusive design could work.

| Question | Analysis prompt |
|---|---|
| Necessity | Can the feature work with fewer or less precise signals? |
| Expectation | Would users reasonably expect this use in this context? |
| Vulnerable users | Could teens or at-risk users face greater harm? |
| Fairness | Could the feature reinforce exclusion or harmful recommendations? |
| Control | Can people understand and change the relevant setting? |
| Accountability | Who owns the risk, and how will outcomes be checked? |

## Documented example
In 2022 Ireland's Data Protection Commission announced a decision imposing a €405 million fine and corrective measures concerning Instagram processing relating to child users. The inquiry examined public disclosure of email addresses and/or phone numbers through business-account features and public-by-default settings at the time examined [1]. Use this as a historic example of why defaults and children's privacy need design review; it does not describe current settings.

## Recommendations and conclusion
Consider age-appropriate defaults, clear audience settings before publication, limits on sensitive inferences, recommendation impact testing, and accessible reporting/appeals. Ethical review asks whether a less intrusive, fairer, safer design is available, in addition to whether a practice meets legal requirements.
